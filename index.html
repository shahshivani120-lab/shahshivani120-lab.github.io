Feature 01 — Organic search + AI visibilitySRA HQ · USA · May 2024 – Sep 2026

The firm Copilot kept quoting.

Survey Research Associates is a 30-year-old US firm serving AmLaw-tier law firms. It had almost no search visibility. Its pages became the source AI assistants cited for buyer questions in its category, often ahead of the software vendors' own sites.

The problem

Law-firm leaders were researching retention, performance reviews and legal AI tools, and SRA wasn't in those answers on Google or in AI assistants.

What I did

  • Rebuilt website content, technical and on-page SEO in Webflow
  • Mapped buyer questions into comparison, buyer-guide and research content
  • Tracked AI citations in Bing Webmaster Tools and doubled down on the formats AI kept quoting

Clicks grew 166% while impressions grew 19%. The right pages, not just more pages.

Feature 03 — Paid social · message testingRestoGenius · Australia · Feb – Nov 2026

17 hooks to find the message café owners reply to.

Busy venue owners rarely fill in forms, but they will reply to a message. So I ran every Meta ad on the messaging objective and treated small budgets as a message test, then put the winning offer behind the Black Friday campaign.
Download the full case study (PDF) ↓

How I ran it

  • One hook per ad, so each cost per conversation shows how well that message worked
  • Small, steady budgets of ₹280–₹500 a day, so a weak hook never burned much money
  • Seven themes: free hardware, a named $89 price, hidden add-ons, fees, menu insights, operational pain, delivery commissions
  • Keep, re-run or retire: winners re-run and tracked, losing themes dropped

Creative fatigue

The audience of Australian venue owners is small, so winners wear out within months. Re-running “Still paying thousands for POS?” 8 days later cost +8%; re-running “$89 all-in-one” three months later cost +160%. So each new push now gets a fresh visual or hook on a proven offer, in Ads Manager where frequency can be watched.

Owners recognise the problem, but they reply to an offer.

Feature 04 — Founder-led LinkedIn ghostwritingBrijesh · SNDK Corp · USA · 17–23 Nov 2026

One week, 73,322 impressions.

I ghostwrite LinkedIn for Brijesh, US-based founder of SNDK Corp, a B2B IT company in cloud and AWS, networking, security and custom software. Topics, hooks and posts are written in his voice, and they travelled far beyond his network to bring buyers to his profile.
Download the full case study (PDF) ↓

How I work

  • Voice: learn how he talks about his work, clients and running an IT company
  • Topics: a bank of technology decisions, client lessons and founder stories buyers relate to
  • Hooks & drafts: one idea per post, with a first line that earns the “see more” click
  • Publish: under his name, so readers connect with the founder, not a brand page
  • Learn: impressions, comments and profile views decide which topics to repeat

What I'd do next

Track 4+ weeks and log which posts lead to calls, add follow and button calls to action (followers grew under 1% despite the reach), and test carousels and other formats people repost.

53 comments for every 100 reactions: people had something to say back to a founder.
